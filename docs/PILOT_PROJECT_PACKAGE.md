## Overview

This document provides a pilot project package for testing and validating Cooling Credits in real-world settings.

The goal is not to begin with a national-scale program.

The first step is to start with measurable small-scale pilots: schools, parks, station plazas, shopping streets, public facilities, farms, forests, waterfronts, and coastal areas.

These pilots measure air temperature, humidity, WBGT, surface temperature, cooling demand, soil moisture, vegetation, rainwater use, reclaimed-water use, and organic-matter circulation, then connect the results to Cooling Scores and provisional Cooling Credits.

---

## Basic Approach

A Cooling Credit pilot should follow this sequence:

```text
Start small
↓
Measure
↓
Visualize cooling impact
↓
Check maintenance cost
↓
Convert to Cooling Score
↓
Connect to business models
↓
Evaluate scalability
```

The goal is not to build a perfect market from the beginning.

The goal is to create measurable and repeatable pilot models that companies, municipalities, researchers, and local communities can join.

## Standard Pilot Durations

### 30-Day Pilot

A 30-day pilot tests short-term heat mitigation, installation effects, and measurement methods.

Example sites:

```text
schoolyards
station plazas
bus stops
shopping streets
public-facility entrances
event sites
small parks
```

Measured items:

```text
air temperature
humidity
WBGT
surface temperature
user perception survey
cooling-device operating time
water use
electricity use
```

---

### 90-Day Pilot

A 90-day pilot covers a summer season, a post-rainy-season heat period, or a dry season.

Example sites:

```text
public facilities
station redevelopment areas
urban parks
schools, hospitals, welfare facilities
farm plots
shopping districts
```

Additional measured items:

```text
cooling electricity demand
soil moisture
vegetation condition
rainwater or reclaimed-water use
maintenance cost
visitor count
heat-stress indicators
```

---

### One-Year Pilot

A one-year pilot evaluates seasonal change, maintenance, and broader natural-function response.

Example sites:

```text
municipal public-facility networks
watershed forests
farms and orchards
coastal fisheries
tourism areas
urban green networks
```

Additional measured items:

```text
annual cooling demand
annual water use
soil organic matter
vegetation index
simple biodiversity indicators
post-rainfall water retention
maintenance system
project revenue and cost
```

## Pilot Types

### 1. Urban Cooling Pilot

Purpose: reduce heat-island stress, pedestrian heat exposure, and cooling demand.

Examples:

```text
mist cooling
shade structures
water-retentive pavement
rainwater use
wall greening
rooftop greening
center-mist ultrasonic cooling fans
```

Key indicators:

```text
air temperature reduction
WBGT reduction
surface temperature reduction
cooling demand reduction
user count
maintenance cost
sustainable water source
```

---

Purpose: restore soil water retention, microbial activity, and carbon fixation capacity.

Examples:

```text

organic-matter circulation

rainwater infiltration
microbial recovery
```

Key indicators:

```text
soil moisture
soil temperature
organic matter
crop condition
surface temperature
infiltration
water-retention time
```

Key indicators:

```text
forest-floor temperature
soil moisture

```

### 4. Coastal and Fishery Recovery Pilot

Key indicators:

```text
water temperature
dissolved oxygen
turbidity
nutrients
biomass
fishery-related indicators

```

## Connection to Cooling Score

Each pilot converts measured values into a Cooling Score.

Examples:

```text
air temperature reduction
WBGT reduction
surface temperature reduction
cooling demand reduction
soil moisture recovery
vegetation recovery
organic-matter circulation
water-cycle recovery
maintenance continuity
```

These values can be combined into provisional Cooling Credit units.

At the pilot stage, they should be treated as provisional evaluation units, not as formal financial products.

---

## Success Conditions

A pilot should not be judged only by whether temperature decreases.

Success should be evaluated across multiple conditions:

```text
measurable impact
repeatability
maintenance feasibility
sustainable water source
no major negative local impact
cost-effectiveness
local acceptance
connection to business or institutional scaling
```

---

## Cautions

Cooling Credit pilots should avoid exaggerated claims.

Avoid:

```text
This will definitely reduce global mean temperature.
More clouds always cool the planet.
Cooling actions permit more emissions.
Credits can be issued without measurement.
```

Use:

```text
Measure local and regional heat-load reduction.
Evaluate recovery potential of natural cooling functions.
Assess impacts on water cycles, soils, forests, and ocean circulation.
Implement alongside emissions reduction.
```
