## Overview

This directory organizes Cooling Credits not only as a climate-countermeasure concept or technical framework, but as a set of **planetary rescue business models** that can involve nations, municipalities, companies, investors, fishery communities, forest owners, farmers, and local societies.

Previous related repositories mainly focused on:

- direct cooling technologies,
- marine circulation recovery,
- urban cooling,
- soil regeneration,
- forest regeneration,
- organic-resource circulation,
- desert regeneration,
- Civilization OS and Natural Complementary Science.

However, social implementation requires more than technological possibility.

It must clarify who invests, who operates, who benefits, what returns to the region, what is measured through MRV, and how the resulting cooling contribution can be evaluated as Cooling Credits.

This directory provides that business-model layer.

---

## Core Philosophy

Cooling Credit business models are based on the following principle:

```text
From a society where destroying nature is profitable,
to a society where restoring nature is profitable.
```

Waiting for a full transformation of human consciousness may be too slow.

Cooling Credits therefore do not deny economic desire. They redirect it toward natural regeneration, heat-load reduction, water-cycle recovery, food security, disaster-risk reduction, and regional economic revitalization.

## Business Models

### 1. EEZ Fishery Recovery Cooling Credit Business Model

- [EEZ Fishery Recovery Cooling Credit Business Model](EEZ_FISHERY_RECOVERY_COOLING_CREDIT_MODEL.md)

A business model that combines OBS, OTU, and UMC/UMS to evaluate oxygen supply, vertical circulation, surface-heat suppression, plankton-foundation recovery, fishery-recovery potential, food security, and coastal economic revitalization within Exclusive Economic Zones.

This model does not guarantee fishery catch increases. It evaluates whether marine environmental improvement can increase recovery potential through staged pilots and MRV.

---

### 2. Tourism Resource Recovery Cooling Credit Model (Planned)

A business model for regenerating coral reefs, beaches, lakes, forests, cool-climate destinations, and urban waterfronts through natural cooling, water-cycle recovery, landscape restoration, and ecosystem regeneration.

---

### 3. Desert Circular Pyramid City Business Model (Planned)

A model for converting deserts into cooling, food, tourism, research, and residential assets through RO water, reclaimed water, organic-resource circulation, humus production, fruit trees, shade, mist cooling, and pyramid-shaped water-circulation urban design.

---

### 4. Urban Green Infrastructure and Cooling Model (Planned)

A municipal and corporate model integrating parks, street trees, rooftop greening, rainwater use, water-retentive pavement, urban farms, mist cooling, and organic humus circulation to reduce urban heat load.

---

### 5. Monoculture Mountain Forest to Native-Fruit Mixed Forest Business Model (Planned)

A model for converting monoculture or abandoned mountain forests into zoned systems of fruit orchards, wild edible forests, mushroom forests, nectar forests, evergreen forests, deciduous broadleaf forests, wildlife habitats, and watershed forests, restoring cooling, carbon fixation, water circulation, biodiversity, food production, and tourism value.

## Common Evaluation Axes

Each business model may be evaluated through combinations of:

- air-temperature reduction,
- surface-temperature reduction,
- WBGT improvement,
- soil water-retention recovery,
- plant evapotranspiration recovery,
- water-cycle recovery,
- surface-ocean heat suppression,
- dissolved oxygen improvement,
- biodiversity recovery,
- organic-resource circulation,
- food-production stabilization,
- regional economic revitalization,
- tourism-resource recovery,
- disaster-risk reduction,
- MRV data quality.

---

## Common Caution

The business models in this directory do not guarantee effects with 100% certainty.

Fishery catch, tourism revenue, greening effects, cooling effects, carbon-fixation effects, and ecosystem-recovery effects vary depending on local conditions, climate conditions, ecosystem conditions, implementation scale, management quality, and MRV quality.

These models are institutional designs for evaluating high-potential cooling, regeneration, and economic-circulation models through measurement, verification, and staged pilots.
