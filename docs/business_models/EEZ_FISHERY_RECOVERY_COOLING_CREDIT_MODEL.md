## Overview

This model redefines the Exclusive Economic Zone (EEZ) not only as a space for fishing rights or marine jurisdiction, but as a **Cooling Credit business model for marine regeneration, fishery-recovery potential, food security, coastal economic revitalization, and ocean cooling contribution**.

It combines marine and water-cycle technologies such as OBS (Ocean Breathing System), OTU (Ocean Tuning Unit), and UMC/UMS (Ultrasonic Mist Cooling / Mist System) to evaluate the following possibilities:

- surface-ocean heat-load reduction,
- assisted vertical circulation,
- dissolved oxygen improvement,
- normalization of nutrient circulation,
- recovery potential of non-harmful plankton foundations,
- improvement potential of fishery habitats,
- coastal fishery, seafood-processing, and regional economic recovery,
- reduction potential for marine heatwave, harmful algal bloom, and hypoxia risks.

This model does **not** guarantee a 100% increase in fishery catch.

Marine ecosystems are complex. Fishery catch depends on water temperature, oxygen, nutrients, plankton, currents, spawning grounds, overfishing, pollution, climate change, and transboundary fish migration.

This model is a business design for improving marine environmental conditions, measuring that process through MRV, and evaluating the recovery potential as a Cooling Credit.

## 1. Background

The ocean is already under multiple stresses:

- rising sea surface temperature,
- marine heatwaves,
- deoxygenation,
- harmful algal blooms,
- dead zones,
- disrupted nutrient circulation,
- coastal pollution,
- overfishing,
- degradation of spawning grounds, seagrass beds, tidal flats, and coastal habitats,
- changes in plankton foundations.

Declining fishery resources are not only a problem of fewer fish.

They affect coastal employment, food security, regional culture, seafood processing, tourism, port economies, and the entire marine ecosystem.

Conventional fishery policies have focused on catch regulation, stock management, aquaculture, no-take zones, and release programs.

These remain important. However, if the ocean's heat, oxygen, circulation, and ecological foundation are degraded, catch regulation alone may not be sufficient.

This model therefore repositions the EEZ as a cooling asset, oxygen asset, food asset, and regional economic asset.

## 2. Basic Concept

The basic structure is as follows:

```text
Marine environment within the EEZ
↓
OBS / OTU / UMC-UMS for circulation, oxygen, and surface-heat measures
↓
MRV of marine environmental data
↓
Evaluation of fishery-recovery potential and marine cooling contribution
↓
Cooling Credit conversion
↓
Reinvestment into fisheries, tourism, seafood processing, and coastal economies
```

This is not a model for selling a device that magically increases fish.

It is a model for measuring marine environments, improving cooling, oxygen, circulation, and ecosystem foundations, and connecting that improvement potential to regional economic recovery.

## 3. Core Technologies

### 3.1 OBS: Ocean Breathing System

OBS is a concept for supporting vertical circulation, dissolved oxygen, and nutrient circulation through deep or mid-water aeration and microbubble rise.

Expected roles:

- possible mitigation of hypoxic water masses,
- assistance of vertical mixing,
- promotion of exchange between surface and lower layers,
- potential improvement of plankton foundations,
- support for the ocean's breathing function.

Cautions:

- excessive aeration may affect ecosystems,
- design must reflect local depth, currents, oxygen state, and nutrient state,
- monitoring and stop conditions are required to avoid stimulating harmful algal blooms.

---

### 3.2 OTU: Ocean Tuning Unit

OTU is a marine-tuning unit concept that integrates surface heat, deep water, aeration, spiral flow, reverse rotation for cold-water upwelling, and mist-cooling support.

Expected roles:

- possible local suppression of surface sea temperature,
- limited use of deeper cold water,
- assistance of vertical circulation,
- possible mitigation of local marine heatwave risk,
- support for oxygen and nutrient circulation.

Cautions:

- limited pilots are required before any larger deployment,
- ecosystem impact monitoring is essential,
- regional stratification, currents, and fishery structure must be considered.

---

### 3.3 UMC / UMS: Marine Mist and Water-Cycle Cooling

UMC/UMS uses ultrasonic mist and evaporative cooling to reduce heat load above the sea surface or around coastal facilities.

Expected roles:

- local heat-load reduction near the sea surface,
- heat countermeasures for ports, aquaculture facilities, and coastal tourism areas,
- combination with ocean cooling technologies,
- improvement of tourism resources and working environments.

Cautions:

- effectiveness may be limited in high-humidity environments,
- water quality, salt damage, equipment durability, and electricity use must be evaluated.

## 5. Revenue Structure

This model combines multiple sources of value rather than relying on a single revenue stream.

### 5.1 Cooling Credit Revenue

Marine heat-load reduction, port and coastal cooling, marine heatwave-risk reduction, and oxygen improvement can be evaluated through MRV and converted into Cooling Credits.

### 5.2 Fishery Revenue Recovery Potential

If marine environmental improvements contribute to resource recovery, fishery catch, fish quality, fishery stability, and regional brand value may improve.

This is not guaranteed. Long-term observation and stock management are required.

### 5.3 Seafood Processing and Regional Branding

Regions can be branded as regenerative fisheries, cooling-contribution fishing grounds, or marine-regeneration regions.

### 5.4 Tourism, Education, and Research Revenue

Marine regeneration projects can support ecotourism, ocean education, corporate training, research hubs, and regional public relations.

### 5.5 ESG, CSR, and National Strategy Investment

Companies and governments can participate not as donors, but as investors in measurable ocean cooling, fishery recovery potential, and coastal economic revitalization.

## 7. Implementation Steps

### Step 1: Marine Area Diagnosis

Survey water temperature, oxygen, currents, nutrients, catch, harmful algal blooms, hypoxia, seagrass beds, tidal flats, and fishery structures within the target EEZ or coastal region.

### Step 2: Small-Scale Observation Project

Do not introduce equipment immediately. First integrate observation buoys, sensors, satellite data, and fisher-provided data.

### Step 3: Limited Pilot

Pilot OBS, OTU, or UMC/UMS elements that fit local conditions, with limited scale, limited duration, and stop conditions.

### Step 4: MRV Evaluation

Measure effects on water temperature, oxygen, ecosystems, fisheries, and regional economies, including both cooling contribution and side effects.

### Step 5: Stepwise Expansion

Only when benefits are confirmed and side effects remain acceptable, expand the area, duration, or number of units step by step.

### Step 6: Cooling Credit Conversion

Based on MRV results, evaluate marine cooling contribution, oxygen improvement, marine heat-risk reduction, and fishery-recovery potential as Cooling Credits.

### Step 7: Regional Reinvestment

Reinvest credit revenue into fisher support, seagrass restoration, tidal-flat restoration, port infrastructure, ocean education, and next-generation fisheries.

## 9. Risks and Constraints

This model includes the following risks:

- effects may be limited depending on the marine region,
- unexpected ecosystem impacts may occur,
- harmful algal blooms or specific species increases may be triggered,
- fishery stakeholder coordination is required,
- typhoons, storms, and salt damage can affect equipment,
- initial investment and maintenance costs may be high,
- effects cannot be judged without long-term observation.

Therefore, this model must not assume large-scale deployment from the beginning. It requires phased pilots, stop conditions, third-party evaluation, and local agreement.

## 12. Summary

An EEZ is not merely a marine jurisdiction.

It is a national asset supporting food, security, fisheries, tourism, ocean circulation, carbon circulation, heat circulation, and ecosystems.

Traditionally, EEZs have often been discussed in terms of fishing rights or seabed resources.

From the Cooling Credit perspective, an EEZ can be reevaluated as a **cooling asset, oxygen asset, food asset, and ecosystem asset**.

OBS, OTU, and UMC/UMS are not magical devices that guarantee fishery catch increases.

However, they are candidate technologies for measuring and improving marine heat, oxygen, circulation, and plankton foundations, thereby increasing the potential for fishery recovery.

This model is an institutional design for converting that potential into a business framework.

```text
Evaluate the EEZ not only as a fishing ground,
but also as a cooling asset, food asset, and marine regeneration asset.
```

This is the EEZ Fishery Recovery Cooling Credit Business Model.
