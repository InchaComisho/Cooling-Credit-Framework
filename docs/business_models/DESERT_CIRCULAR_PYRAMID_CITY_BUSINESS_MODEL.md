## Overview

This model redefines deserts not as unusable land, but as **national regeneration assets integrating water circulation, food production, cooling, tourism, research, and habitation**.

It combines RO desalination, reclaimed water, organic-resource circulation, humus production, fruit trees, shade, ultrasonic mist, solar power, wind power, small hydropower, and pyramid-based urban structures to gradually cool, green, and inhabit drylands.

This is not a plan to green an entire desert at once. It is a staged business model that pilots and expands circular city units while evaluating water resources, salinity, climate, soil, energy, and maintenance.

---

## Basic Concept

```text
Desert or dryland
↓
RO water, reclaimed water, rainwater capture, organic-resource circulation
↓
Humus production, fruit trees, shade, mist, water-channel cooling
↓
Habitation, food, tourism, research, and cooling assets
↓
MRV of water-cycle, cooling, and vegetation recovery
↓
Cooling Credit conversion
```

## Core Components

### 1. Water-Cycle Infrastructure

- RO desalination,
- advanced wastewater treatment,
- rainwater capture,
- reclaimed-water irrigation,
- small channels and reservoirs,
- water-quality monitoring.

### 2. Organic-Resource Circulation

- food-waste humus production,
- composting of pruning branches and leaves,
- incineration reduction,
- soil carbon increase,
- water-retention recovery.

### 3. Vegetation Design

- fruit trees,
- dryland-adapted trees,
- shade trees,
- windbreak forests,
- nectar plants,
- urban farms.

### 4. Cooling Design

- ultrasonic mist,
- water-channel cooling,
- shade structures,
- evapotranspiration cooling,
- reflective and heat-shield materials,
- wind corridors.

### 5. Pyramid-Based Urban Structure

Pyramid or octahedral structures are treated as urban design tools for integrating solar power, wind, shade, airflow, rainwater capture, and vertical water circulation.

## Risks and Cautions

This model does not promote unlimited desert greening.

Excessive water use, salinization, groundwater depletion, invasive species, poor maintenance, and excessive energy use must be avoided.

Desert regeneration must proceed step by step within the regional water budget, salinity conditions, climate, culture, and land-use constraints.
