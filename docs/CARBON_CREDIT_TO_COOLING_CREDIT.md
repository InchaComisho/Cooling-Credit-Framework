## Overview

Carbon credits have developed as a system for offsetting emitted CO2 through reductions, removals, avoidance, or absorption elsewhere.

Cooling Credits propose a different axis of evaluation: the measurable reduction of actual heat load.

```text
Carbon Credit
= accounting adjustment of emissions

Cooling Credit
= evaluation of physical heat-load reduction
```

This distinction is critical.

If carbon credits are centered on carbon accounting, Cooling Credits are centered on cooling action.

In this sense, Cooling Credits can become a market design for physically reducing planetary heat load and may serve as a foundation for a planetary rescue business.

---

## 1. Limits of Carbon Credits

Carbon credits have value as an institutional tool.

They have encouraged emissions reduction, forest conservation, renewable energy deployment, and carbon removal or absorption.

However, their basic structure is still book-based offsetting.

```text
CO2 is emitted
↓
A reduction, removal, or avoidance is credited elsewhere
↓
The emission is offset in accounting terms
```

This structure has several limitations.

- It is difficult to see whether regional heat actually decreases.
- Urban heat, surface temperature, and WBGT are not directly evaluated.
- Soil water retention, evapotranspiration, and forest cooling capacity are not fully captured.
- Offset systems can become financialized.
- Accounting figures can become more important than physical field changes.
- Citizens often cannot feel the effect directly.

Carbon accounting alone does not directly address accumulated heat load, urban heat, dry soils, lost evapotranspiration, marine heatwaves, or degraded forests.

## 2. Core Idea of Cooling Credits

Cooling Credits evaluate actions that actually reduce heat.

The evaluation target is a measurable physical change, such as:

- Air-temperature reduction
- Surface-temperature reduction
- WBGT improvement
- Humidity-risk management
- Soil water-retention recovery
- Plant evapotranspiration recovery
- Multi-layer forest recovery
- Organic-waste incineration reduction
- Humus production
- Water-cycle recovery
- Urban cooling
- Possible suppression of surface ocean heat risks
- Waste-heat reduction

Cooling Credits evaluate the fact that heat was reduced.

```text
Reduce heat physically
↓
Measure it
↓
Record it
↓
Verify it through MRV
↓
Evaluate it as a Cooling Credit Score
↓
Convert it into Cooling Credits
```

## 3. From Book-Based Offsetting to Physical Cooling

The core transition is:

```text
From book-based offsetting to physical cooling.
From carbon accounting to cooling action.
```

In carbon-credit systems, climate action can remain confined to emissions numbers.

In Cooling Credits, climate action returns to the field.

- Did the road surface temperature decrease?
- Did WBGT improve in this shopping street?
- Did soil moisture increase on this farm?
- Did this forest recover evapotranspiration?
- Did this region reduce organic-waste incineration?
- Did this city reduce waste heat?
- Did this ocean region reduce surface-heat risk under monitored conditions?

Cooling Credits can evaluate actual physical changes in specific places.

## 8. Complementarity with Carbon Credits

Cooling Credits do not need to completely reject carbon credits.

They serve different roles.

| Item | Carbon Credit | Cooling Credit |
|---|---|---|
| Core metric | CO2 emissions | Heat-load reduction |
| Main structure | Book-based offsetting | Physical cooling action |
| Evaluation target | Emission reduction and absorption | Temperature, WBGT, water cycle, evapotranspiration, soil, forests, urban cooling |
| Visibility | Often difficult to feel | Easier to measure and experience locally |
| Participants | Often centered on large companies and governments | Open to companies, municipalities, regions, and citizens |
| Weakness | Financialization and offset dependence | Requires strong MRV and regional correction |

If carbon credits organize emissions accounting, Cooling Credits increase field actions that reduce heat.
