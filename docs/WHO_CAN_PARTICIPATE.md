# Who Can Participate in Cooling Credits?

[← Back to Cooling Credit Framework README](../README.md)

---

## Overview

Cooling Credits are not only for researchers, governments, or environmental organizations.

To measure, value, implement, and maintain real-world cooling actions, many types of participants are needed.

This document explains how investors, municipalities, companies, researchers, local communities, agriculture, fisheries, and tourism sectors can participate in the Cooling Credit Framework.

---

## Basic Structure

```text
Investors
= provide capital

Municipalities
= provide public spaces and policy pilots

Companies
= provide technology, installation, maintenance, and operations

Researchers
= provide measurement, verification, and modeling

Local communities
= implement small-scale actions in daily living areas

Agriculture, fisheries, and tourism
= connect natural cooling-function recovery to business value
```

Cooling Credits connect these roles into one market and institutional framework.

---

## 1. Investors

### Why Participate?

Cooling Credits can connect climate adaptation, urban cooling, natural regeneration, disaster-risk reduction, ESG, and infrastructure investment.

Potential investment areas include:

```text
urban cooling infrastructure
public-facility cooling
water-cycle infrastructure
soil restoration projects
forest regeneration projects
organic-matter circulation projects
coastal and fishery recovery projects
heat-risk reduction projects
```

### What to Evaluate

```text
cooling impact
measurability
maintenance cost
local demand
municipal partnership
disaster-risk reduction
revenue model
credit potential
```

### First Entry Points

- Fund 30-day, 90-day, or one-year pilots
- Co-develop pilots with municipalities
- Invest in cooling infrastructure companies
- Design regional resilience funds

## 2. Municipalities

### Why Participate?

Municipalities face heatwaves, heat stress, heavy rainfall, drought, wildfire risk, cooling demand, and public-facility heat issues.

Cooling Credits can turn these challenges from simple expenses into measurable regional cooling investments.

### Target Sites

```text
schools
parks
station plazas
bus stops
shopping streets
city halls
libraries
hospitals
welfare facilities
evacuation shelters
watershed forests
farmland
coastal zones
```

### First Entry Points

- 30-day pilots at public facilities
- WBGT reduction pilots at schools, parks, and stations
- Rainwater and reclaimed-water cooling pilots
- Link with disaster-prevention and heat-adaptation budgets

## 4. Researchers, Universities, and Research Institutions

### Why Participate?

Cooling Credits require measurement, verification, modeling, regional correction, seasonal correction, and ecological impact assessment.

Researchers support the credibility of the framework.

### Research Themes

```text
WBGT reduction assessment
surface temperature change
soil moisture recovery
evaporation and evapotranspiration assessment
urban heat-environment modeling
water-cycle recovery modeling
forest cooling effects
ocean circulation support effects
MRV design
Cooling Score design
```

### First Entry Points

- Measurement design for pilots
- Baseline setting
- Third-party verification
- Academic papers and modeling
- Joint research with municipalities and companies

---

## 5. Citizens and Local Communities

### Why Participate?

Cooling Credits are not only for large infrastructure projects.

Small local cooling actions can improve regional heat environments if they are measured and maintained.

### Examples

```text
rainwater use
home and local greening
composting
humus production
shade creation
shopping-street mist systems
water-retention improvement in parks
school heat adaptation
```

### First Entry Points

- Record WBGT and perceived temperature
- Compare surface temperature before and after implementation
- Record photos before and after greening
- Test cooling actions at community events
- Propose pilots to municipalities

---

## 7. First Step to Participate

Participation does not require a complete market from the beginning.

The first step is to measure small actions.

```text
1. Select a site
2. Select a time period
3. Select measurement items
4. Implement cooling actions
5. Compare before and after
6. Convert results to Cooling Score
7. Consider business or institutional scaling
```
